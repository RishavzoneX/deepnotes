Life Processes

Comprehensive notes covering all essential topics related to nutrition, respiration, transportation, and excretion in living organisms.

Nutrition

Autotrophic Nutrition

Autotrophic nutrition is the process by which organisms produce their own food using inorganic materials like CO₂ and water. This process is primarily carried out through photosynthesis in green plants.

Photosynthesis: The process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy, synthesizing organic compounds from inorganic materials.

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Requirements for Photosynthesis:

  • Carbon Dioxide: Enters through stomata in leaves
  • Water: Absorbed by roots from soil
  • Sunlight: Energy source for the process
  • Chlorophyll: Green pigment that traps light energy

Stomata

Stomata are microscopic pores found on the surface of leaves and stems that facilitate gas exchange and transpiration.

Functions of Stomata:

  • Exchange of gases (O₂ and CO₂) between plant and atmosphere
  • Transpiration - loss of water vapor from plant surfaces
  • Regulation of temperature through evaporative cooling

Heterotrophic Nutrition

Heterotrophic nutrition is the mode of nutrition where organisms obtain food from other sources since they cannot synthesize their own food.

Types of Heterotrophic Nutrition:

Type Description Examples
Holozoic Ingestion and digestion of solid food Humans, animals
Saprophytic Feeding on dead and decaying matter Fungi, bacteria
Parasitic Deriving nutrition from host organisms Tapeworms, Cuscuta

Human Digestive System

The human digestive system consists of the alimentary canal and associated glands that work together to break down complex food substances into simpler absorbable forms.

Key Organs and Functions:

  • Mouth: Mechanical digestion through chewing
  • Stomach: Secretes gastric juice for protein digestion
  • Small Intestine: Complete digestion and absorption
  • Liver: Produces bile for fat emulsification
  • Pancreas: Secretes digestive enzymes
  • Large Intestine: Water absorption and feces formation

Respiration

Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells break down glucose to produce energy in the form of ATP. This process occurs in three main stages: glycolysis, Krebs cycle, and electron transport chain.

Types of Respiration:

Type Oxygen Requirement Energy Yield End Products
Aerobic Respiration Requires oxygen 36 ATP per glucose CO₂ + H₂O
Anaerobic Respiration No oxygen required 2 ATP per glucose Lactic acid or Ethanol + CO₂

Human Respiratory System

The human respiratory system facilitates the exchange of gases between the body and the environment, providing oxygen to cells and removing carbon dioxide.

Respiratory Pathway:

  • Nostrils: Air enters the body
  • Nasal Passage: Air is filtered and warmed
  • Pharynx: Common passage for air and food
  • Larynx: Voice box containing vocal cords
  • Trachea: Windpipe leading to bronchi
  • Bronchi: Branch into smaller bronchioles
  • Alveoli: Site of gas exchange

Breathing Mechanism

Breathing involves two processes: inhalation (taking in oxygen-rich air) and exhalation (expelling carbon dioxide-rich air).

Mechanism of Breathing:

Process Inhalation Exhalation
Diaphragm Contracts and flattens Relaxes and becomes dome-shaped
Rib Cage Moves upward and outward Moves downward and inward
Lung Volume Increases Decreases
Air Pressure Decreases Increases

Transportation

Human Circulatory System

The circulatory system in humans consists of the heart, blood vessels, and blood that work together to transport substances throughout the body.

Components of Blood:

  • Plasma: Liquid matrix containing water, proteins, and dissolved substances
  • Red Blood Cells (RBCs): Carry oxygen using hemoglobin
  • White Blood Cells (WBCs): Provide immunity against pathogens
  • Platelets: Essential for blood clotting

Double Circulation

Human circulation is called double circulation because blood passes through the heart twice during one complete cycle:

Circulatory Pathways:

  • Pulmonary Circulation: Heart → Lungs → Heart
  • Systemic Circulation: Heart → Body → Heart

Transport in Plants

Plants have specialized tissues for transporting water, minerals, and food throughout their structure.

Plant Transport Tissues:

Tissue Function Direction of Flow Components
Xylem Transports water and minerals Roots → Leaves Tracheids, vessels
Phloem Transports food and nutrients Leaves → Other parts Sieve tubes, companion cells

Transpiration:

The process of water loss from plant surfaces, primarily through stomata. Transpiration helps in:

  • Creating a suction force for water absorption
  • Transporting minerals from roots
  • Cooling plant surfaces

Excretion

Human Excretory System

The excretory system removes nitrogenous wastes from the body and maintains osmotic balance. The main organs include kidneys, ureters, urinary bladder, and urethra.

Structure and Function of Nephron:

The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney where urine formation occurs through three processes:

  • Glomerular Filtration: Filtration of blood under pressure in glomerulus
  • Tubular Reabsorption: Reclaiming useful substances from filtrate
  • Tubular Secretion: Adding wastes to the filtrate

Urine Formation

Urine formation involves the following steps:

  1. Blood enters the kidney through renal artery
  2. Filtration occurs in the glomerulus
  3. Filtrate passes through renal tubule
  4. Reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, and water
  5. Secretion of urea and other wastes
  6. Formation of urine in collecting duct
  7. Urine passes to ureter → urinary bladder → urethra

Excretion in Plants

Plants eliminate waste products through various methods:

  • Gaseous waste (O₂, CO₂) diffuses through stomata
  • Excess water removed through transpiration
  • Waste products stored in leaves that fall off
  • Deposition in bark which peels off
  • Secretion of resins, gums, and latex

One-Shot Revision Notes

Nutrition

  • Autotrophs: Self-nourishing (photosynthesis)
  • Heterotrophs: Depend on others for food
  • Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
  • Stomata: Gas exchange and transpiration
  • Human digestion: Complex process involving multiple organs

Respiration

  • Aerobic: With oxygen (36 ATP)
  • Anaerobic: Without oxygen (2 ATP)
  • Respiratory pathway: Nostrils → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Alveoli
  • Alveoli: Site of gas exchange
  • Breathing: Inhalation (active) and exhalation (passive)

Transportation

  • Double circulation: Blood passes heart twice per cycle
  • Blood components: Plasma, RBCs, WBCs, Platelets
  • Xylem: Water transport in plants
  • Phloem: Food transport in plants
  • Transpiration: Water loss creates suction force

Excretion

  • Human excretory organs: Kidneys, ureters, bladder, urethra
  • Nephron: Functional unit of kidney
  • Urine formation: Filtration → Reabsorption → Secretion
  • Dialysis: Artificial blood filtration
  • Plant excretion: Through stomata, leaf fall, storage
